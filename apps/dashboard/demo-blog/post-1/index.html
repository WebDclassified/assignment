Why page views still matter

October 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Every analytics conversation eventually returns to the humblest metric of all: somebody opened a page. It sounds trivial until you try to count it honestly — exactly once per navigation, never doubled by a restless framework, never lost when the reader leaves mid-sentence.

One navigation, one event

The rule is simple to state and easy to violate. Browsers fire load events, single-page routers swap content without loading anything, and the back button restores pages from a cache that never tells your script. A tracker earns its keep by emitting one page view per document the visitor actually saw — and staying quiet otherwise.

Count what was seen, not what was requested.

Clicks need a home address

A click without coordinates is a rumor. Recording the position relative to the whole document — plus the size of the viewport and the page at that moment — turns a dot on a heatmap into a statement about a button, a headline, or a photograph that caught someone's eye.

Sessions are a courtesy

Grouping a visit's events under one anonymous identifier lets you read a journey as a story instead of a pile of timestamps. Thirty quiet minutes is a reasonable place to end the chapter and start a new one.
